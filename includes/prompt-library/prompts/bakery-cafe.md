# Hearth & Crumb Bakery — sourdough bakery & café landing page

Design and build a **complete, production-quality landing page** for "Hearth & Crumb Bakery", a neighbourhood sourdough bakery with a small café. **You own the design.** If web search is available, research 2–3 genuinely well-designed bakery or café sites first and form a point of view; then decide the layout, composition, and section design yourself. Do not fall back to a generic centered-hero + three-identical-cards template — bring editorial composition, asymmetry, scale contrast, and rhythm. **Vary your hero composition to fit this brand specifically — do not reach for a full-bleed photo with centered white text, and do not stack image cards over one another; choose an opener that reads like the front of a good food magazine, not a template.** The sections below define **what the page must communicate, not how it must look.**

## Style guide (constraints, not layout)

**Palette — flat colors only, no gradients:**

| Role | Value |
|---|---|
| Surface, light | `#FBF6EE` (cream) |
| Surface, dark bands | `#2B1D14` (espresso) |
| Ink / headings | `#2B1D14` |
| Accent — use sparingly: one word, prices, buttons, the "today" marker | `#F2C14E` (butter) |
| Muted body text | `#7A6A5C` |
| Hairlines / dividers | `#E8DFD2` |

**Typography:** Display = **Fraunces** (fallback: Playfair Display) — a soft, slightly wonky serif; use its italic for one word per headline. Body = **Work Sans**, 16–18px, line-height ~1.7. Menu prices set in the display face at body size so they read as part of the typography, not a spreadsheet.

**Feel:** flour-dusted and warm — crust, crumb, and morning light carried by photography. Generous whitespace; hairline rules between menu items; corners square except on buttons (4px). Nothing glossy.

**Motion:** slow and gentle — fade-up reveals only, staggered on the menu rows. Use only what the chosen builder provides natively; **no custom JavaScript and no hand-rolled motion CSS.**

## Design signature (make this page distinct — adapt as you see fit)
These are compositional cues, not layout mechanics. Use them to give this bakery its own look rather than a generic template:
- **Hero:** a split — a 5-column type block on cream (small uppercase eyebrow, a two-line serif headline with one italic word, the opening hours set as a short colophon line) beside a 7-column full-height photograph of loaves on a rack. Text never sits on the photo.
- **Menu:** a printed price list in two columns with dotted leaders running to right-aligned prices, section headings in small caps, and one item per day marked "today" in butter yellow — no product cards, no photos per item.
- **Process:** three steps set as a horizontal timeline on the espresso band, with the times (4am, 6am, 7am) as oversized numerals.
- **Shape language:** square corners, hairlines over shadows, one dark band, and the butter accent reserved for prices, buttons and the "today" marker.

## Sections — what each must communicate (design them your way)

1. **Hero** — the sense of a real bakery that opens early: the brand name, a memorable headline, a supporting line, the hours today, primary + secondary actions (Order for pickup / See the menu).
2. **Proof bar** — four facts: Baked from 4am daily · 100% naturally leavened · Local organic flour · Est. 2013.
3. **Today's bakes** — the full menu below, grouped Breads / Pastries / Café, with prices; treat it as a designed object (a printed price list, not a card grid).
4. **How it's made** — three steps: 48-hour cold ferment, shaped by hand at 4am, baked in the stone-deck oven by 6am; short, factual, no adjectives doing the work.
5. **Cakes & events** — custom cakes and pastry boxes for events, with 3 days' notice, and a working order-enquiry form (name, email, phone, date needed, occasion, guests, notes) built with the builder's native form element; a phone number as the alternative. If the builder has no form element, use a clearly labelled call/email call-to-action instead of omitting this.
6. **Reviews** — three distinct, believable customer quotes with first names; write them yourself.
7. **Visit** — address, hours per day, phone, email, parking note, and a map if the builder supports one.
8. **Newsletter** — one line inviting people to hear which loaves are on this week; a single email field.
9. **Footer** — brand + tagline, quick links, contact, copyright.
