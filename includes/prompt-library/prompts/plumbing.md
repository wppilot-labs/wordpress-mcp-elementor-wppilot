# RapidFlow Plumbing — 24/7 plumbing & emergency repair landing page

Design and build a **complete, production-quality landing page** for "RapidFlow Plumbing", a residential plumbing company with a 24/7 emergency service. **You own the design.** If web search is available, research 2–3 genuinely well-designed trade or home-service sites first and form a point of view; then decide the layout, composition, and section design yourself. Do not fall back to a generic centered-hero + three-identical-cards template — bring editorial composition, asymmetry, scale contrast, and rhythm. **Vary your hero composition to fit this brand specifically — a person with a burst pipe is reading this on a phone at 2am; the number must be the biggest thing on the screen, the hero must have no photograph competing with it, and there must be no hero slider.** The sections below define **what the page must communicate, not how it must look.**

## Style guide (constraints, not layout)

**Palette — flat colors only, no gradients:**

| Role | Value |
|---|---|
| Surface, light | `#FFFFFF` |
| Surface, dark bands | `#0B1F3F` (deep navy) |
| Ink / headings | `#0B3D91` (blue) |
| Accent — urgency bar, the phone number, buttons, the emergency band's numerals | `#E4322B` (alert red) |
| Muted body text | `#586174` |
| Hairlines / dividers | `#DEE2EA` |

**Typography:** Display = **Oswald** (fallback: Bebas Neue), uppercase, weight 600, tight leading — the phone number set in Oswald at the largest size on the page. Body = **Roboto**, 16–18px, line-height ~1.6. Prices in Oswald.

**Feel:** fast, direct, high-contrast — thick 3px rules, square corners, big numerals, no decoration. Photography only in the "who arrives" section (a real technician and a lettered van) and nowhere near the hero.

**Motion:** none that delays reading; a quick fade on section entry at most. Use only what the chosen builder provides natively; **no custom JavaScript and no hand-rolled motion CSS.**

## Design signature (make this page distinct — adapt as you see fit)
These are compositional cues, not layout mechanics. Use them to give this company its own look rather than a generic template:
- **Urgency bar:** a full-width red strip above the header: "Burst pipe or no hot water? Call now — a plumber is on the way in 60 minutes" with the number as a link.
- **Hero:** on white, the phone number **is** the headline — one line, oversized, tappable — with "24/7 · 60-minute response · No overtime charge" as the eyebrow above it, one sentence beneath, and two actions (Call now / Book online). Beside it, a tight vertical checklist of what is covered, each line with a red check.
- **Services:** a two-column list with a small SVG icon, service name, one line, and a flat price in Oswald — no cards, hairlines between rows.
- **Emergency band:** three numbered steps on deep navy (call, we dispatch, we fix and price before we start) with the numerals oversized in red.
- **Shape language:** square corners, 3px rules, one navy band, red reserved for the bar, the number, buttons and numerals.

## Sections — what each must communicate (design them your way)

1. **Urgency bar** — the number and the promise, in one line.
2. **Hero** — a company that answers the phone: the brand name, the phone number as the headline, the three-part promise, one sentence, Call + Book actions, and the coverage checklist.
3. **Proof bar** — four facts: Licensed AZ ROC #318844 · 24/7, 365 days · 60-minute emergency response · 5,000+ jobs completed.
4. **Services** — all eight services below with one line each and a flat or starting price.
5. **What happens when you call** — the three emergency steps, plus the no-overtime and price-before-work promises.
6. **Pricing, plainly** — the call-out fee, what it includes, that it is waived when work goes ahead, and that there is no overtime, weekend or holiday surcharge.
7. **Who arrives** — a photo of a technician and a lettered van, background-checked, uniformed, shoe covers; write it plainly.
8. **Reviews** — three distinct, believable customer quotes with first names and neighbourhoods; write them yourself.
9. **Book online** — a working booking form (name, phone, address, what's wrong, how urgent: emergency / today / this week, preferred time) built with the builder's native form element, with the phone number given equal prominence. If the builder has no form element, use a clearly labelled call call-to-action instead of omitting this.
10. **Service area** — the cities served, hours (24/7), phone, email, and a map if the builder supports one.
11. **Footer** — brand + tagline, quick links, contact, license number, copyright.
