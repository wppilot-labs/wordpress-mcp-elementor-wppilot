# Greenline Landscaping — landscaping & lawn care landing page

Design and build a **complete, production-quality landing page** for "Greenline Landscaping", a residential landscaping and lawn-care company. **You own the design.** If web search is available, research 2–3 genuinely well-designed landscaping or outdoor-trade sites first and form a point of view; then decide the layout, composition, and section design yourself. Do not fall back to a generic centered-hero + three-identical-cards template — bring editorial composition, asymmetry, scale contrast, and rhythm. **Vary your hero composition to fit this brand specifically — this is the one brief where a large photograph belongs in the hero, but the type must be anchored to a solid block at the bottom-left, never floated in the middle of the image, and there must be no overlapping image cards.** The sections below define **what the page must communicate, not how it must look.**

## Style guide (constraints, not layout)

**Palette — flat colors only, no gradients:**

| Role | Value |
|---|---|
| Surface, light | `#F6F4EF` (stone) |
| Surface, dark bands and type blocks | `#1E1B17` (soil) |
| Ink / headings | `#1E1B17` |
| Accent — use sparingly: buttons, season tags, numerals | `#4C8C3F` (leaf) |
| Muted body text | `#6F6B63` |
| Hairlines / dividers | `#DCD8CF` |

**Typography:** Display = **Archivo** (fallback: Anton), weight 900, uppercase for headlines, tight tracking; big and blunt. Body = **Source Sans 3**, 16–18px, line-height ~1.7. Eyebrows in small uppercase with wide tracking.

**Feel:** outdoors and physical — big photographs with hard square edges, heavy type, honest materials. No rounded corners, no shadows, no leaf illustrations or clip-art; the plants are in the photos.

**Motion:** sturdy — a single slide-up on section entry, nothing that floats. Use only what the chosen builder provides natively; **no custom JavaScript and no hand-rolled motion CSS.**

## Design signature (make this page distinct — adapt as you see fit)
These are compositional cues, not layout mechanics. Use them to give this company its own look rather than a generic template:
- **Hero:** one full-bleed photograph of a finished garden, with the headline set uppercase on a soil-coloured type block anchored bottom-left that bleeds off the page edge; the "free estimate" button sits inside that block, and the supporting line sits below the image on stone, not over it.
- **Services:** alternating rows — photograph left, text right, then flipped — each with a leaf-green season tag (Spring / Summer / Fall / Year-round), a two-sentence description, and a starting price; no icon cards.
- **Before & after:** three paired photographs side by side with "Before" and "After" labels set as small uppercase on the images' top corners.
- **Shape language:** square everything, hairlines over boxes, one soil band for the numerals, leaf green only on tags, buttons and numbers.

## Sections — what each must communicate (design them your way)

1. **Hero** — a company that builds real gardens: the brand name, a memorable uppercase headline, one supporting line, the free-estimate action, and the service area in one phrase.
2. **Proof numerals** — four facts on the soil band: 15 Years · 1,200+ Yards · 3-Year Plant Warranty · Licensed & Insured.
3. **Services** — all six services below with descriptions, a season tag, and starting prices, as alternating photo rows.
4. **Before & after** — three project pairs with a one-line caption each (neighbourhood and what changed); write the captions yourself.
5. **Care plans** — the three seasonal plans below, what each includes, and monthly pricing; one is marked most popular.
6. **How it works** — four steps: site visit and estimate within 5 days, a drawn plan, install with a named crew lead, care plan hand-off.
7. **Reviews** — three distinct, believable homeowner quotes with first names and neighbourhoods; write them yourself.
8. **Get an estimate** — a working estimate-request form (name, phone, email, property address, service needed, budget range, notes) built with the builder's native form element, with the phone number given equal prominence. If the builder has no form element, use a clearly labelled call/email call-to-action instead of omitting this.
9. **Service area & hours** — the towns served, office hours, phone, email, and a map if the builder supports one.
10. **Footer** — brand + tagline, quick links, contact, license number, copyright.
