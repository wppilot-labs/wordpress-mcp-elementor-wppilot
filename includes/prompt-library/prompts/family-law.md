# Whitlock & Reyes Family Law — boutique family law firm landing page

Design and build a **complete, production-quality landing page** for "Whitlock & Reyes Family Law", a two-attorney family law practice. **You own the design.** If web search is available, research 2–3 genuinely well-designed small law firm sites first and form a point of view; then decide the layout, composition, and section design yourself. Do not fall back to a generic centered-hero + three-identical-cards template — bring editorial composition, asymmetry, scale contrast, and rhythm. **Vary your hero composition to fit this brand specifically — no gavel, no scales, no handshake, no skyline, no photo behind the headline; the hero is a typographic statement with a single portrait, and the tone throughout is steady rather than combative.** The sections below define **what the page must communicate, not how it must look.**

## Style guide (constraints, not layout)

**Palette — flat colors only, no gradients:**

| Role | Value |
|---|---|
| Surface, light | `#F7F4EE` (cream) |
| Surface, dark bands | `#22252A` (charcoal) |
| Ink / headings | `#22252A` |
| Accent — use sparingly: the index numerals, links, one word | `#1F4D3A` (deep green) |
| Muted body text | `#6A6E74` |
| Hairlines / dividers | `#DDD9D0` |

**Typography:** Display = **Newsreader** (fallback: Lora), regular weight with italic for emphasis — set large with tight leading; never bold. Body = **Inter**, 16–18px, line-height ~1.7. Numerals for the index in Newsreader at display size.

**Feel:** restrained and humane — a lot of cream, one photograph of each attorney and no others, hairlines, square corners, no shadows, no icons. The page should read like a well-set letter, not a billboard.

**Motion:** a single soft fade on section entry; nothing else. Use only what the chosen builder provides natively; **no custom JavaScript and no hand-rolled motion CSS.**

## Design signature (make this page distinct — adapt as you see fit)
These are compositional cues, not layout mechanics. Use them to give this firm its own look rather than a generic template:
- **Hero:** a 12-column grid on cream: the serif statement headline spans eight columns at the largest comfortable size, with one italic phrase; the right three columns hold a single portrait of the two partners and, beneath it, the consultation offer as two lines of text with a text link — no buttons, no background image.
- **Practice areas:** a numbered index — 01 to 06 in deep green Newsreader numerals down the left, the area name and two sentences to the right, hairlines between; if the builder has a native accordion, each row may expand to a third sentence on fees.
- **Proof:** three facts set as a single line of small caps between hairlines — words, not oversized numerals.
- **Shape language:** square corners; hairlines over boxes; one charcoal band for the process; deep green reserved for numerals, links and one word.

## Sections — what each must communicate (design them your way)

1. **Hero** — a firm that is calm when clients are not: the firm name, a statement headline, one sentence on how they work, the portrait, and the consultation offer.
2. **Proof line** — three facts: Colorado family law only, since 2011 · Flat fees where the law allows · Named attorney on every matter.
3. **Practice areas** — all six areas below, as a numbered index with two sentences each.
4. **How we work** — three short paragraphs: settlement first, litigation when needed, and what "you will always know where your case stands" means in practice (weekly written updates).
5. **Attorneys** — the two partners with a photo, bar admission, and one line each on background; write the lines from the facts below.
6. **What a first consultation looks like** — four steps on the charcoal band: a 20-minute call, a 60-minute meeting, a written options letter within 3 business days, no obligation.
7. **Fees** — the flat-fee list below and the hourly rate for everything else; state that estimates are in writing.
8. **Reviews** — three distinct, believable client quotes with first initials only (privacy); write them yourself, understated.
9. **Request a consultation** — a working form (name, phone, email, matter type, preferred contact method, a few words about your situation) built with the builder's native form element, with a confidentiality note beneath it. If the builder has no form element, use a clearly labelled call/email call-to-action instead of omitting this.
10. **Office** — address, hours, phone, email, parking, and a map if the builder supports one.
11. **Footer** — firm name, quick links, contact, copyright, and the disclaimer line below.
